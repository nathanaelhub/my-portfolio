## The brief

Most residents learn about a rezoning from a sign in a yard, a week before the hearing. The records
are public, but they're spread across Metro Planning's Development Tracker, the Board of Zoning
Appeals and Metro Codes, each with its own map. This puts them on one page for one address. Live at
[nathanaelhub.github.io/nashville-near-me](https://nathanaelhub.github.io/nashville-near-me/), with a
mirror on [Vercel](https://nashville-near-me.vercel.app).

## The build

- **Address to parcel** through Metro's parcel layer, then four public ArcGIS layers queried around
  it: open planning cases, zoning appeals, permit applications in review and permits issued in the
  last year.
- **Upcoming Planning Commission and zoning-appeal hearings first**, soonest at the top, with dates
  shown in Nashville time.
- **Linked to the [Nashville Council Digest](https://nathanaelhub.github.io/nashville-council-digest/)**: a planning case that
  became a council bill links to that bill's fact-checked, plain-language page.
- **Static site, no dependencies**: plain HTML and ES modules, tested with Node's built-in test runner.

## What I learned

The privacy rules matter more than the features. The page never requests owner, appellant or contact
fields from Metro's layers, and a test fails if a query asks for one. Phone numbers and emails in
free-text permit notes are blanked, which first also blanked 10-digit permit numbers until the
pattern required separators. And a section that's usually empty (about 50 zoning appeals a year
countywide) needed checking to show it was the data, not a bug.

## Stack

JavaScript (ES modules), Metro Nashville ArcGIS REST services, Node test runner, GitHub Pages, Vercel.
