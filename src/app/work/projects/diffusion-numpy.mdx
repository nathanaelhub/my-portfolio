## The brief

After a GPT and an MLP by hand, the next step was generation: a diffusion model where every layer's
forward and backward pass is written out, trained on an 8 GB MacBook.

## The build

- **Two networks with one interface**: a 4.1M-parameter MLP and a 152k-parameter U-Net (3×3
  convolutions by im2col, GroupNorm, skip connections), both gradient-checked in float64.
- **v-prediction, a cosine schedule, class conditioning** with classifier-free guidance, Adam with
  warmup and an EMA of the weights; checkpoints resume bit-for-bit.
- **An evaluation without Inception**: a separately trained MNIST classifier (97.3% test accuracy)
  supplies conditional accuracy and a Fréchet distance on its features.

## What I learned

The U-Net is 27× smaller and saw 6× fewer images, but it beat the MLP at every noise level:
Fréchet 42.5 against 177.2 conditionally, 95.3 against 236.8 unconditionally. Guidance raises
accuracy but past a point collapses each digit to one stereotyped shape. I also corrected my own
README: I had called the training images' score the floor, but the classifier was trained on them,
so held-out test images are the right reference, and two slices of them differ by 19 points.

## Stack

Python, NumPy, MNIST, uv, pytest.
