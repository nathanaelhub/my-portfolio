## The brief

Small nonprofits are adopting AI tools without training. A one-hour session, run by someone who
isn't a technologist, can change the four habits that matter most. See it at
[nathanaelhub.github.io/nonprofit-ai-workshop](https://nathanaelhub.github.io/nonprofit-ai-workshop/).

## The build

- **Everything a facilitator needs**: a minute-by-minute guide, slides with speaker notes, four
  exercises with answer keys, a one-page privacy sheet, a quiz to give before and after, a
  feedback form and a runbook for keeping it current.
- **Real stakes in the exercises**: the SNAP income limit and Tennessee's unresolved 200% question
  from my benefits screener; fictional intake emails full of private details to practice removing.
- **Accuracy enforced by tests**: every statement about how Anthropic handles chats is quoted from
  its Privacy Center and checked against a cached copy; attendee pages must read at grade 8 or
  below; the hour must add up to 60 minutes.
- **A pre-workshop check**: `workshop check` runs each exercise through Claude three times and
  scores the answers in code, so the facilitator knows what Claude says that week. About $0.10.

## What I learned

My spec predicted Claude would invent numbers when asked vaguely for a grant paragraph. It didn't:
every time, it left blanks like "[X] households" and asked for the real figures. The answer keys now
say what actually happened, and the lesson moved to the person filling in the blanks. My first
scoring of those answers was wrong too, until I read them. The kit hasn't been run with real staff
yet, and says so.

## Stack

Markdown and a small Python build, Claude (Sonnet through the Claude CLI), pytest, GitHub Pages.
