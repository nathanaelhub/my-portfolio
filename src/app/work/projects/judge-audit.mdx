## The brief

In an eval audit for open-finance-mcp, a Claude Haiku judge with three votes per verdict failed
answers that met every clause of their rubric, and did so unanimously. Adding votes is the usual
fix. The question was whether it would have helped.

## The build

- **Known-answer items**: responses whose correct verdict is fixed in advance, plus paraphrases,
  table-instead-of-prose rewrites and padded versions of each.
- **Accuracy split by direction**: false fails (a good answer failed) and false passes.
- **Vote independence**: the intraclass correlation of errors, and the number of independent votes
  that *k* majority votes are actually worth.
- **Invariance and agreement**: whether a rewording changes the verdict, and Cohen's κ between
  judges (Haiku, Sonnet and Opus).

## What I learned

The unanimous errors were dataset errors. Haiku's mistakes were locked to items (error ICC 0.80), so
three votes were worth 1.2 independent ones. Reading the judges' reasoning showed the items were at
fault: one rubric asked for a link the response didn't give, and one item's accession numbers were
ones I had made up. After fixing the items, all three judges reached 100%. Item-locked errors are a
signal to check the item, not to add votes.

## Stack

Python, Claude (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus), statistics in NumPy.
