## The brief

Year-over-year changes in a 10-K's risk factors are where companies disclose new worries, but
the documents are long and the edits are scattered. Demo reports for Apple, NVIDIA, JPMorgan Chase
and Coca-Cola: [nathanaelhub.github.io/10k-diff](https://nathanaelhub.github.io/10k-diff/).

## The build

- **Find the filings** through EDGAR's submissions API, including heavy filers like JPMorgan whose
  "recent" page holds one 10-K among thousands of note prospectuses.
- **Flatten the inline-XBRL HTML** into paragraphs, dropping hidden metadata and page furniture, and
  rejoining paragraphs a page break cut in two.
- **Split into Items** using only headings outside tables, so the table of contents doesn't count.
- **Align by content, not position**, so reordered risk factors aren't reported as changes:
  unchanged, figures updated, reworded, merged or split, added or removed.

## What I learned

Matching is heuristic, so I checked it by hand across four companies with different formatting: 41
of all 45 borderline rewordings (91%) and 18 of 20 random ones from the next band up (0.60–0.75) were matched correctly, with
every error written up. For Apple's FY2025 report it found the sourcing language changing from
"Substantially all" to "A significant majority", a new paragraph on age-verification laws, and the
removal of the "Inclusion and Diversity" section.

## Stack

Python standard library, SEC EDGAR APIs, `difflib`, GitHub Pages.
