## The brief

Nonprofits rewrite official letters for the people they serve, and reading-level scores reward
cutting. Earlier work in this portfolio found that AI judges catch a changed number but miss a dropped
condition. This measures what plain language costs, letter by letter. Results at
[nathanaelhub.github.io/plain-language-bench](https://nathanaelhub.github.io/plain-language-bench/).

## The build

- **18 public-domain federal notices** with placeholders filled in, and 571 facts (448 a reader
  needs to act) written to a rule and frozen by hash before any rewrite ran.
- **Eight setups**: Haiku, Sonnet and Opus at grade 6, Sonnet at grades 4 and 8, a "keep every fact"
  prompt, the rewrite tool, and the original letter as a control.
- **A judge tested before it was trusted**: errors planted into real rewrites with labels set by
  construction, pass marks set in advance, and the one error type that missed its mark (must to may,
  6 of 9 caught) published with a warning.
- **A rewrite tool** (`plainbench rewrite`) that extracts the letter's facts, rewrites, checks
  every one, revises once, and reports a checklist; it caught 31 of 35 planted losses. `--redact`
  removes Social Security, claim and case numbers and addresses before anything is sent.

## What I learned

At grade 6 Claude kept about 95–96% of the critical facts. Pushing to grade 4 cost 6 more points,
and 93% of grade-4 letters lost at least one; that's the only difference clearly larger than chance.
Bigger models, the careful prompt and the tool all scored a little better, but by less than the 4.6
points the same setup varies between runs. The judge has limits I published next to every number: it
agreed with only 1 of 5 subtle real changes I found by hand, such as an appeal that had to be "in
writing". A person's blind labels on 40 items are the next check. About $26 of Claude usage in all.

## Stack

Python, Claude (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus through the Claude CLI), pytest, bootstrap and Wilson
intervals, GitHub Pages.
